Axial Flux BLDC Motor

Introduction

Axial flux brushless DC (BLDC) motors offer a compact alternative to conventional radial flux motors, with magnetic flux flowing parallel to the motor's rotational axis. Axial Flux has the benefits of compactness and adjustable air gap and is easier to fabricate for a DIY project. In this project I made a prototype of a coreless axial flux BLDC motor. Why coreless? Since it was a DIY project, I wanted to keep the cost low. It also had the added benefits of being lightweight. A Halbach Array was used to focus the magnetic flux on one side.

Design

i) Rotor:

  • Number of Poles: The rotor is designed to have 12 poles with 24 N35 grade neodymium magnets arranged in Halbach array.
  • Halbach array: Generally, a BLDC motor has magnets with alternating poles. Since, I opted for a coreless design, it is necessary to maximise the magnetic flux density in the air gap. To achieve this,I used a Halbach array. In this configuration, magnets are positioned with polarity at 0, 90, 180… degrees. This concentrates the magnetic field on one side and weakens the other side. The trade-off is that this needs twice the number of magnets than a conventional alternating pole arrangement, increasing the overall cost.
  • Dimensions:
    • Inner radius: 9.5 mm
    • Outer radius: 40 mm
    • Depth: 10 mm
  • Material Used: 3D printed with PLA plastic.

ii) Stator:

  • Number of Poles: The stator is designed to have 18 coils with 30 turns per coil. The coils are connected in Y configuration. So, total turns is 18 x 30= 540 turns.
  • Dimensions:
    • Radius: 40 mm
    • Depth: 12 mm
  • Material Used: 3D printed with PLA plastic.

Components

  • Neodymium magnets (N35): 10x10x2mm (24pcs)
  • Enameled copper wire: 24SWG
  • ESC 30A: for testing
  • Battery Pack: 3S Lipo (compatible with the ESC)
  • Arduino Uno: Microcontroller for PWM signal generation
